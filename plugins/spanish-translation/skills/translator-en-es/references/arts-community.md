# Visual arts and community arts translation

Domain-specific conventions for translating open calls, residency applications, artist statements, gallery materials, and community arts programming. Read alongside `style-guide.md`.

## Terminology: displaying and showing work

- **`exponer`** is the standard verb for exhibiting artwork in LatAm art contexts — not `exhibir`. Use `exponer` in all narrative sentences.
  - ✅ `los artistas que exponen aquí`
  - ❌ `los artistas que exhiben aquí`
- **`su obra`** (or `su trabajo`) refers to an artist's body of work. Never use the generic `arte` when the artist's specific work is meant.
  - ✅ `un lugar donde mostrar su obra`
  - ❌ `un lugar para mostrar arte`
- **`instalación`** is the correct term for installation art. `Desmontaje` is standard for de-installation/takedown. `Montaje` for installation/setup.
- **`boceto`** for artistic sketches (not `esbozo`). `Maqueta` for scale models/mockups. `Propuesta` for the overall artistic proposal.
- **`portafolio`** (LatAm) for portfolio — not `carpeta` (Spain) or `dossier`.
- **`colectivo artístico`** for an artist collective or team — more natural than `equipo de artistas`.
- **`taller`** is preferred over `estudio` for a studio/workspace in community arts contexts — `taller` implies active making, `estudio` implies a private professional space.
- **`inauguración`** for an opening reception or opening night — not `recepción inaugural`.
- **`egresado/a`** for a recent graduate (LatAm standard). Not `graduado/a` (anglicism) or `titulado/a` (Spain).

## Naming conventions: residency titles vs. narrative

- In **headers, labels, and formal references**: use the full title `Artista en Residencia (AIR)`.
- In **narrative sentences**: use `artista residente` (lowercase, natural in running text).
  - ✅ `el artista residente trabajará en la creación de una instalación`
  - ❌ `el Artista en Residencia trabajará en una instalación` (overly formal mid-sentence)

## Passive voice preference

Spanish strongly prefers the reflexive passive (`se + verb`) over the periphrastic passive (`ser + past participle`) in narrative and descriptive contexts.

- ✅ `Se invita a los artistas a considerar...` / `la instalación que se expondrá`
- ❌ `Los artistas son invitados a tratar...` / `la instalación que presentará` (active sounds like the artist is presenting a business pitch)

For invitations and instructions addressed to artists, `Se invita a X a + infinitive` is the most natural construction.

## "The X itself" constructions

Use `la/el propia/o + noun` rather than `la/el + noun + misma/o` for "the X itself" — the former is more natural in LatAm narrative prose.

- ✅ `como parte de la propia historia`
- ❌ `como parte de la historia misma`

## Listing themes and subjects

When listing thematic subjects after `temas`, use `como` + **definite article** for each item — not bare nouns introduced by `de`.

- ✅ `temas como la lucha, la historia, el lugar y el proceso`
- ❌ `temas de lucha, historia, lugar y proceso` (reads like a category tag list, not flowing prose)

## Identity and inclusion language

### Form field phrasing
- Use `¿Cómo te identificas?` for identity self-identification fields — direct, natural, widely used in inclusive arts contexts.
- Avoid `¿Cómo eliges identificarte?` — the added `eliges` is well-intentioned but sounds slightly clunky in Spanish.

### Identity terms (arts/activist register)
- **`queer`**: keep in English — universally self-used across LatAm LGBTQIA+ communities; any Spanish rendering changes the register.
- **`BIPOC`**: keep in English as an acronym; translate individual terms in checkboxes.
- **`Negro/a o Afrodescendiente`**: combined form — `Negro/a` is reclaimed in many communities; `Afrodescendiente` is the formal/institutional term. Use combined form when audience is pan-LatAm. May be adjusted for specific regional context.
- **`no conforme con el género`**: preferred over `no binario/a de género` for "gender non-conforming" — the former is a direct rendering of the source concept.
- **`que se identifica como mujer`**: correct for "female-identifying" — preserves the self-identification frame.

## Register

Community arts open calls directed at emerging artists (especially LGBTQIA+/BIPOC audiences) use **`tú`** throughout — warm, peer-level, community-facing. Do not default to `usted` in this domain even though it's a formal application.
