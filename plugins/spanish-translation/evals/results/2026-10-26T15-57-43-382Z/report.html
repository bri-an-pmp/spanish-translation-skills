1. **"del Distrito Federal" (notary's designation), possible error in the source.** Mexico's Federal District was officially renamed Ciudad de México in 2016. It doesn't include any limits on the power, its term, revocation terms, the notary's closing or authorization, or any apostille. US banks checking signing authority usually want a translation of the complete certified copy (*testimonio*), often with an apostille (Mexico is party to the Hague Apostille Convention). The certification above says "excerpt" to be accurate. If the bank needs a full certified translation, send the whole instrument.

3. **Whether she can sign checks or promissory notes, a point for the bank's counsel.** Under Mexican law (Ley General de Títulos y Operaciones de Crédito, Art. 9), the power to issue, endorse or guarantee negotiable instruments (*títulos de crédito*) generally must be granted expressly. A general power under Art. 2554 may not cover it on its own. This excerpt doesn't mention *títulos de crédito*. If the bank needs Ms. Torres Núñez to sign checks, notes or similar instruments, it should check whether the full deed grants that power. This is a legal point I'm raising for review, not a legal opinion.

4. **Capacity in which the power is granted.** In this excerpt, Mr. Ramírez de la Vega grants the power in his personal capacity. It doesn't say he is acting for a company. If the bank account belongs to a company, this power would not by itself give authority over it.

5. **Seals and signatures.** Your text already describes the seal and signatures in brackets, so I translated those descriptions and couldn't transcribe what the seal actually says. A certified translation made from the original would transcribe the seal's text and note any handwriting or initials in the margins.

6. **"la señora" → "Ms."** I chose "Ms." because the deed doesn't state Ms. Torres Núñez's marital status. "Mrs." is the traditional rendering if the bank or agency prefers it.

7. **Long-form date and numbers.** I kept the long-form date ("the twelfth day of the month of March of the year two thousand twenty-four") and the written-out article number, as is standard in certified translations. 8. **Certification must be signed by a person.** The name, date and signature in the certification block are placeholders. A qualified translator has to review the translation against the original document and sign it.
